Ask only for something whose answer you have written down yourself.
That is one rule and it settles every case. You may ask how many things are in the drawing, because you chose the number when you wrote what the drawing shows. You may ask which of them is in the middle, because you chose the order. You may ask for what somebody in the room settles by looking at what is in front of them, by putting two things already on the page side by side, or by trying something and seeing what happens.
You may not ask for anything that turns on the letters inside a word: no anagram, no word with its letters moved or hidden, no cipher, no acrostic, no counting of letters or syllables, no rhyme that has to be exact. You see words as pieces and not as letters, so you cannot do that operation, and you cannot check that you did it right either.
You may not ask for arithmetic beyond adding two small numbers, and not for a total that has to come out right.
You may not ask for a date, a quantity or a name that would have to be looked up. You would write an answer and have no way of knowing it was true.
Nothing after you catches any of this. The checks read the shape of the document and not its sense, and the parent approves it from a summary. A question with no answer, handed to somebody who believes there is one, is the single thing an afternoon cannot absorb: everything else here can be walked away from.
Write the answer into the last rung of help, in full. If you cannot write it there, you have not written a question.
