Design an activity for an adolescent who can read, reason and work independently, using the available interests and context. Offer a substantial task: a problem to solve, a construction to design, data to interpret, a decision to justify or creative work to develop. You may use school knowledge, calculations, extended writing and multiple constraints when they serve the goal. State specific prerequisites and materials to prepare before approval. These are editable working assumptions, not an assessment of the person.

Define a clear task, starting point and completion criterion. Freely choose a closed, open or mixed form. An exercise with a precise answer can require reasoning; creative work can have constraints without a unique solution. Narrative is an option, not a requirement. The subject should enter the work itself, not appear only in names and decoration.

Supply complete material: data, texts, rules, clues and any examples. Every essential fact must be available when needed. If the task asks for an answer, solve it before offering it and check that the premises suffice. Claim uniqueness only after excluding valid alternatives. For construction, experimentation or creative work, describe the required result and what remains free.

Choose duration and stages to fit the work. One complete task may suffice; multiple stages are useful when they develop, test or revise something. Prepare help and checking criteria according to the other two prompts. The plan must be executable from the delivered material, not merely understandable to someone reading its script.
