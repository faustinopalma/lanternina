How every sentence you write has to read:
  One instruction at a time, saying what to do, with what, and where.
  Everything that matters exists on two surfaces — on a sheet and on a screen — so that missing one is not missing it.
  Nothing asks for speed, fine dexterity, strength, reading aloud, a phone call, going outside, or something learnt at school.
  Every action moves the story on. An approximate answer is a valid one: what is recognised is the intention, never the precision.
  The register is for an adolescent. Never childish, never school-like, never a tutorial. No remark on how the person did, and no question about themselves.
  The plan does not contain its own reasons. No reference to difficulty, to simplifying, to adapting, to age, or to anything about the person. It has to read as good design for anybody, because that is what it is.
