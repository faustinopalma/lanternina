# XIAO camera firmware

This project targets the assembled XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, USB serial 94:A9:90:D0:9D:D0, with 8 MiB flash and 8 MiB PSRAM. The shutter connects D1/GPIO2 to ground. The external LED connects D4/GPIO5 through its series resistor to ground. D3/GPIO4 and the former LED output D2/GPIO3 are inputs with neither digital nor RTC pull-up/pull-down. Keep unused wire ends disconnected and insulated. The microphone is not initialized.

Revision `camera-2026-09-09-d1-d4` was compiled and installed on 9 September after the owner reported moving the shutter to D1 and the LED to D4. It uses GPIO2 for awake presses and EXT0 wakeup, with its RTC pull-up enabled before sleep, and GPIO5 for LED feedback. The temporary PIN_TEST mode is removed and the 30 ms debounce is unchanged. Flash hashes, mounted filesystem and fresh authenticated HTTP 200 telemetry were verified. USB diagnostics showed GPIO2 HIGH with pull-up, GPIO4 without pulls, and GPIO5 configured as an output at LOW. Button presses, visible LED feedback and battery sleep/wake still require physical acceptance. The D3 fault is not being investigated further at the owner's request.

The espressif32 PlatformIO platform 6.10.0 and Arduino ESP32 2.0.17 build this project on lanternina hub. Its package directory is `/srv/lanternina/tools/camera-platformio`; builds are isolated under `/srv/lanternina/build/cameras/<MAC without colons>`. The existing PlatformIO executable is reused without upgrading it; e-paper packages and builds remain in their original directories.

## Build And Flash

The root-only `/etc/lanternina/camera.json` configuration contains a per-camera token, TLS paths and the family gallery credential. The build header is generated on the hub by `deploy/setup-camera.py`; it is excluded from Git. Firmware binaries contain credentials and remain in the protected build directory.

Run `sudo env PYTHONPATH=/opt/lanternina python3 deploy/flash-camera.py --mac 94:A9:90:D0:9D:D0 --hub-address 192.168.0.158` from `/opt/lanternina` for an update. This is the entry point for enrollment and flashing; add `--first-install` only for a new board whose filesystem may be initialized. `--build-only` detects the board, verifies its backup and compiles without uploading. `--build-user` selects the local compiler account and defaults to fausto. The hub needs Python venv support, OpenSSL, the lanternina group and Wi-Fi configuration in `/etc/lanternina/trmnl-provisioning.json`. Missing camera tooling is bootstrapped in an isolated venv. The hub address must be reserved in DHCP. The existing pinned certificate is reused; changing its host requires deliberate certificate rotation and rebuilding all cameras.

The certificate includes the numeric host as both an IP SAN and a DNS SAN because the installed Arduino/mbedTLS verifier rejected an IP-only SAN. This compatibility fix was tested on the board: the authenticated status request returned HTTP 200. Use `--renew-certificate` only when explicitly rotating the certificate, then rebuild and reflash every camera that pins it. Individual camera tokens remain unchanged.

The command locks camera provisioning, verifies the selected USB MAC, ESP32-S3 chip and 8 MiB flash before writing, requires a complete protected backup and regenerates the selected camera's credential header for every build. Each camera keeps its existing token; another camera receives a separate token and build directory. Shared tokens are rejected. Updates omit `uploadfs`. Repeated first-install requests are refused after installation. Post-flash checks require the USB identity, a mounted filesystem and fresh authenticated telemetry. The original backup is `/var/lib/lanternina/camera-backups/94A990D09DD0.bin`, 8,388,608 bytes. A replacement camera follows the same command with its own detected MAC; the previous camera remains enrolled until its token is deliberately removed from the protected receiver configuration. Reusing another camera's MAC or binary is not a replacement procedure.

USB-triggered e-paper provisioning uses `--registered-only`. A camera or unknown board is ignored, including a board accidentally entered in the display registry before provisioning completed. Explicit enrollment and reflashing of e-paper displays remain available through `devices.trmnl_provision`.

## Capture And Power

A stable press starts one capture and turns the LED on continuously. Revision `camera-2026-09-09-capture-led` keeps it on until the selected framebuffer has returned from the camera driver; the loop then switches it off, so changing the framing can no longer change that image. Saving and uploading continue with the LED off. Two 150 ms flashes confirm a matching durable hub receipt, separated from acquisition by at least 500 ms of darkness. A failed capture ends the acquisition light and reports three error flashes; light-off alone does not certify storage or delivery. Do not disconnect power until delivery finishes. The same behavior applies to awake presses, button wakeup and explicit USB diagnostic captures.

The capture-first revision starts Wi-Fi only after JPEG capture and verified local storage finish. This removes radio startup from the capture path at the cost of later delivery; it does not remove sensor startup time. Camera initialization occurs for each capture, using the first JPEG framebuffer, 1600 x 1200 output and one PSRAM buffer. Two warm-up frames remain available through the diagnostic `CAPTURE_SETTLED` command. Exposure checks across lighting conditions remain necessary.

The capture-light revision compiled and was installed on 9 September, preserving the queue. A diagnostic USB capture observed GPIO5 HIGH during acquisition and LOW after `frameReadyMs=543`, before storage and upload completed. The JPEG contained 39,642 bytes; acquisition plus verified storage took 1,248 ms and upload took 6,617 ms. HTTP 201, matching receipt and queue zero were observed, followed by acknowledged USB_TEST_OFF. GPIO register observations establish software output levels; a physical movement/blur trial and visible LED timing remain separate acceptance checks.

On 9 September the earlier battery-run diagnostics showed capture plus storage taking 2,445 to 2,538 ms and upload taking 588 to 634 ms in the inspected recent cycles. They also showed successive button-triggered deep-sleep wakes with `sleepSeconds=0`, which has one-second resolution. These records confirm repeated GPIO wake events, not duplicate delivery of one image. New `sensorInitMs`, `frameReadyMs` and `storageMs` fields separate sensor initialization, time to the selected JPEG framebuffer and local persistence; `frameReadyMs` is not an exposure timestamp.

The USB comparison measured the first JPEG ready in 543 ms in two captures, versus 831 ms with two warm-up frames. Sensor initialization took 364 to 365 ms. Both originals were inspected in the same scene: the first was darker but usable and retained more highlight detail. This is not evidence for all lighting conditions. The installed first-frame default saved an 80,752-byte JPEG with `captureMs=1873`, `storageMs=1306`, `uploadMs=711`, HTTP 201 and an empty queue. These times exclude deep-sleep boot and physical debounce. Delivery flashes do not mark exposure time.

A fresh USB check of `camera-2026-09-09-photo` measured a 75,932-byte JPEG at 1600 x 1200: sensor initialization 366 ms, framebuffer ready 543 ms, verified storage 1,217 ms, total acquisition and storage 1,776 ms, upload 707 ms. The receiver returned HTTP 201 and the queue reached zero. A subsequent receiver-offline test saved a 77,846-byte JPEG, retained it after a rejected connection and delivered the same identifier once after restart. The complete acceptance command took 77.1 seconds, including the one-minute retry interval and observation. GPIO4 remained LOW; moving the USB retry outside the released-button sleep condition fixed the previously blocked upload. This does not repair the electrical fault.

`USB_TEST_ON` temporarily disables physical button capture triggers while the USB host is active, for diagnostic comparisons on the unstable input. It leaves GPIO readings visible. `USB_TEST_OFF`, USB removal or reboot restores normal triggering. This mode was explicitly turned off after the tests. The following 60.5-second passive observation had no capture starts and sampled GPIO4 HIGH; that short observation does not establish that the intermittent defect is resolved.

For development, the owner authorized explicit USB capture commands on 9 September 2026. `CAPTURE` followed by a newline takes one photograph only while the USB host is active and the camera is idle. `STATUS` reports the state; `STORAGE` writes and removes two small diagnostic files. There is no network capture endpoint. On the hub, `/srv/lanternina/tools/platformio-venv/bin/python /opt/lanternina/tools/camera_usb.py CAPTURE` issues one capture and waits for both a matching receipt and an empty queue. Failed or busy operations exit with an error rather than claiming success.

LittleFS holds at most three pending JPEGs of at most 750,000 bytes each. These are engineering limits, not measured typical image sizes. Writes are read back before promotion. Incomplete files are discarded at boot. Missing or damaged filesystems are not automatically formatted. Failed uploads preserve the JPEG and identifier; acknowledgements must match that identifier before two confirmation pulses and deletion from the device queue.

The filesystem image made by the installed mklittlefs tool refused a 37-character path while a 16-character path succeeded, despite the runtime SDK declaring a 64-character name limit. This was the cause of the first physical captures failing before upload. Local basenames now use 24 hexadecimal characters and check for collisions; the complete 32-character identifier remains in metadata and in every receipt. The filesystem was not reformatted. A second fault was attempting to unlink the metadata while its File object was still open. The file is now closed before receipt cleanup, and a failed metadata deletion preserves the JPEG for retry.

A USB data host is detected from USB frame activity, independently of whether a serial monitor is open. Without a live bus, the device enters deep sleep after work and LED feedback complete and the button is released. GPIO2 wakes it for a capture. A USB charger without data cannot be distinguished from battery power by this method; guaranteed charger detection needs an additional VBUS sensing circuit. No timer wakes the camera on battery. Pending uploads retry at the next capture or while connected to USB.

`sudo env PYTHONPATH=/opt/lanternina /srv/lanternina/tools/platformio-venv/bin/python -m tools.camera_offline_check` performs one explicit diagnostic capture while the camera receiver is briefly stopped, restarts it and checks a single durable receipt plus an empty queue. Its exit path restores the receiver and sends `USB_TEST_OFF`. It does not interrupt power, simulate a full filesystem or repair the shutter. USB diagnostic captures carry their purpose in queue metadata and never advance an activity. `captureRequested` distinguishes a new capture operation from telemetry or retry work that still carries an earlier capture identifier.

When connecting USB while the camera is already asleep, press and release the shutter once to wake it. The firmware then detects the host and stays awake while the cable remains connected. Connecting the cable alone is not a deep-sleep wake source on this wiring. This sequence was confirmed on the hub during the USB capture tests.

The camera synchronizes its clock without capture after a cold boot. If its clock was not set at capture time, the hub archives the image but does not guess which activity it belongs to. Battery deep sleep preserves the RTC; complete power loss does not.

The base board cannot measure its battery without extra wiring. Status reports therefore carry a null voltage. The optional 2:1 divider and disabled `CAMERA_BATTERY_GPIO` setting are documented in `docs/macchina-fotografica/README.md`. USB presence does not prove a battery is charging.

## Hub And Parent Panel

`lanternina-camera.service` receives authenticated HTTPS uploads on port 8443. SQLite commits the JPEG and immutable receipt before success is returned. A background worker passes correctly dated photos to the matching activity moment or renders them for a photo display. Activity changes share a process lock with scanner and timer operations. A worker interrupted after claiming a photo does not replay physical actions automatically; its processing state remains visible for diagnosis.

Activities may use `collect` with `source: camera` for constructions, objects and arrangements, with no preceding printed page required. That path sends one photograph to the reader. A photograph returned to a paper collection moment (`source: scanner`, the default) instead sends two images: the exact original A4 PNG kept for printing, followed by the photograph. The model compares content despite perspective and lighting changes, transcribes legible additions and reports uncertainty instead of guessing missing writing. It does not invoke the separate scanner page-placement task. The scanner remains available. Unknown capture clocks, ambiguous run bindings, expired moments and uncertain readings do not advance the activity. Automated tests cover both image paths; legibility of physically photographed handwriting still requires a trial.

An archived photograph reaches a display only when that display explicitly holds the `photo` job. The `picture` job alone authorizes generated pictures, not camera photographs. Both the camera worker and display server enforce this distinction, including photo files written by an earlier revision. A sleeping display changes its existing pixels on its next connection. On 9 September an incorrect fallback displayed a camera photo on CF7D04 despite both displays holding only picture, sheet and remind jobs; this fallback has been removed.

Run `sudo env PYTHONPATH=/opt/lanternina python3 -m tools.camera_review` to list failed or interrupted work. Inspect the current activity and any already printed material before using `--archive <photo ID>`. The authenticated receiver serializes review against processing, keeps the photograph and closes its processing state without replaying a print or continuation. Deletion wins over a late completion or review. An uncertain result can be addressed by another photograph while the same moment is waiting; interrupted effects must be reviewed first.

Other photographs already queued for the same interrupted moment remain blocked. Reviewing or deleting the interrupted record archives those queued siblings as well, without advancing the activity. The parent's activity controls remain the place to decide what happens after inspecting already executed effects.

The parent panel has a Photographs section. The hub synchronizes originals and processing state through its existing device credential. The family can view, download and delete individual photographs, a confirmed inclusive date range or all photographs in a previewed selection. New photos arriving after the preview are excluded. Deleted identifiers remain as receipts without image bytes to prevent reuploads. Hub copies and photo display files are removed at the next successful synchronization; an offline e-paper device keeps its old pixels until it reconnects. Downloads already made by a person and any infrastructure backups are outside this deletion path.

The Devices section lists each camera by its own identity, with a separately editable name, USB state, last device contact and measured battery voltage when hardware supports it. Cameras currently have no selectable jobs, so their rows do not show a job selector or an unassigned-job label. Several cameras can coexist with distinct credentials and receipts. On the current board battery measurement is unavailable. Sleeping on battery does not generate repeated camera heartbeats.

After rewiring to D1/D4 on 9 September, the owner observed working shutter and LED feedback and took several battery-powered photographs. Three completed capture events in the inspected history had `usb=false`, `previousSleepConfirmed=true`, HTTP 201, matching capture/upload IDs and queue zero. Their preceding sleeps were 118, 135 and 74 seconds, measured by the device RTC at one-second resolution. This confirms those sleep/wake and delivery cycles; it does not establish battery life or exclude every intermittent fault. Presses while the device is busy are ignored without an acceptance flash and are not queued.

## Capture And Sleep Diagnostics

The camera reports `before_upload` and `operation_complete`, carrying the boot identifier, trigger, wake cause and reset reason; capture identifier and JPEG dimensions/bytes; capture/storage, upload and work durations in milliseconds; HTTP result and matching-receipt acceptance; queue size, filesystem usage, free heap and free PSRAM; USB presence, button input and clock validity. The final event reports the queue after receipt cleanup. The hub retains the latest 20 events per camera and synchronizes them to the parent Photographs section under Camera diagnostics. These fields contain device facts and no credentials or image bytes.

Before sleeping, the firmware makes one bounded attempt to report `sleep_planned`, then turns Wi-Fi off. If USB was disconnected while idle, it briefly reconnects to report that transition. An unavailable network cannot require indefinite wakefulness. A held button prevents sleep and is reported as `buttonPressed=true`; the camera remains awake on a live USB host. Diagnostics add network work and are part of the development firmware, so their cost must be included in later battery measurements.

`sleep_planned` is an intention, not proof that sleep occurred. Immediately before deep sleep the firmware stores a marker, boot identifier and wall-clock time in RTC memory. At the next wake it reports `previousSleepConfirmed=true` only if the reset cause is deep sleep and that marker survived. `previousBootId` links the confirmation to the previous cycle; `sleepSeconds` is the RTC elapsed duration when the clock was known, or -1 when unavailable. A cold boot, crash or ordinary reset does not confirm sleep. This evidence verifies the firmware sleep transition; it does not measure current or prove that every peripheral reaches its expected low-power consumption.

On 9 September the instrumented USB test recorded a 160,299-byte JPEG: capture and verified local storage took 2,866 ms, upload took 944 ms, and the final report carried HTTP 201, a matching capture identifier and queue size zero. The deep-sleep confirmation path awaits the next physical battery test.

Later on 9 September, the owner confirmed the shutter was physically released while GPIO4 read LOW. A read-only register check immediately after reset reported raw HIGH, pull-up enabled, pull-down disabled, input enabled and output disabled (IO_MUX 0x1b00). A later check reported raw LOW with the same register values. That transition produced a button-triggered photograph without a USB capture command. This supports investigating the electrical input path; it does not locate a faulty component. Physical capture and sleep acceptance tests are paused until the released contact and wiring are checked independently of the powered board. Do not invert the input polarity to hide this symptom.

`sudo env PYTHONPATH=/opt/lanternina python3 tools/verify_camera.py` checks existing authenticated camera telemetry and creates, duplicates and deletes one synthetic JPEG in the live cloud archive. It does not trigger the camera, alter family photographs or test focus. On 9 September 2026 this check passed against the published API; the API, panel and Python CI workflows for commit f9cd5a4 also succeeded.

Real USB capture tests on 9 September produced 1600 x 1200 JPEGs of 113,136, 134,520 and 178,635 bytes. The last two used the corrected cleanup and returned HTTP 201, matching receipt identifiers and an empty camera queue. The third capture reused the running camera without a reboot. Hub records confirmed synchronization to the parent archive, and an original was decoded and visually inspected. The images remain private, outside Git.
