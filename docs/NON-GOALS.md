# Non-goals

Empty since 2 September 2026, when the old lines were taken out rather than patched: several of them turned out to forbid things nobody meant to forbid. What replaces them comes from the research in [enciclopedia/](../enciclopedia/README.md), form by form, with the reasoning attached.

One line can be written now, because it was decided on 4 September 2026 and it replaces a rule that used to be stated in a dozen places.

## A profile is kept. It is never shown.

The old rule said there was nothing about a person anywhere, and that this was a fact of the types rather than a caution. It has been withdrawn. It was utopian and it prevented the work being done well: an afternoon written without any idea of the level it should be pitched at is an afternoon pitched at nobody, and every mechanism the system had for calibrating — a parent's one-off setting, a list of subjects already covered — is a proxy for the thing it was refusing to hold.

So a profile is kept and updated at system level, on axes still to be defined, and it is what lets a task and an afternoon be corrected towards the person actually doing them.

**Three things did not move, and they are the whole of what is refused now.**

**It reaches the models and nothing else.** The profile is written into the prompt of the agent that devises an afternoon and the agent that runs one, because that is what it is for. It reaches no display, no printed sheet, and no page in the parent's panel. Nothing about how somebody is doing is rendered anywhere a person can read it.

**A review gate is what enforces that, not a hope.** What comes back from a model passes a gate whose job is that the profile does not surface explicitly — no sentence saying this was made easier, no remark on how somebody did, no figure about them. The prompt asking for it is not the protection; the gate is.

**Nothing works by making it hard to stop.** No streak, no daily goal, no run of days, nothing withheld as a reason to come back, no notification triggered by absence. This is a different commitment from the one above and it is unchanged: `tests/test_boundaries.py` refuses that vocabulary in the stored shape, in the panel, in the browser and in the prompts. On the vision side, unchanged too: no face or person detection, no emotion or attention inference from images, no gaze estimation, no biometrics.

**And what the adolescent makes is not archived for anybody.** What they write, scan and photograph is read and not kept. A searchable record of everything somebody produced is the thing that makes them feel watched, and feeling watched changes what they make — which is a direct cost to the only thing this system exists to produce. `WhatCameBack` refuses to be pickled, copied or cached, and that is unchanged.

The open question, written down rather than settled: whether a parent should be able to keep an archive of their child's work. It is an ordinary thing for a parent to want. The reason it is not built is that the choice belongs to the adolescent and not to a setting in a panel, and nobody has designed the way they would give it.
