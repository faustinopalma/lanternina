How an afternoon is devised, and how it is run

A walk through the two loops the system actually turns: one in the cloud that writes an afternoon and waits for a parent, and one in the house that reads the clock, prints paper, and decides what happens next from what comes back on it.

0 The shape, before any detail

Two loops, and they never touch directly. The cloud tier holds a panel a parent reads and writes; it stores decisions and it never starts anything. The house holds a small computer, a printer, a scanner, e-paper displays and a button; it runs on a one-minute timer and it is the only thing that asks.

Every arrow that starts work points from the house to the cloud. A parent approving an afternoon writes a row and stops — no model call, no queued job, nothing woken. The house finds that row the next time it looks. This is a rule, not an accident: it is what keeps a panel unable to reach into a room.

1 Devising — where an afternoon comes from

Nothing is devised on a schedule. The house asks for one only when it has looked and found nothing: no approved afternoon it can run, and nothing already sitting with the parent unread. Press the button below to walk the chain.

What the model is actually sent

The whole prompt is in the repository, rendered: docs/prompts/deviser.txt, 11 608 characters. It is assembled from Markdown files sitting beside the agent — agents/experience_deviser.task.md, .format.md, .rules-head.md and so on — plus the format describing itself from shared/experience_prompt.py, plus the household's own material at the end.

The interests are a starting point, not a fence. agents/experience_deviser.household.md says so in as many words: begin from one of them when one fits, and you may go elsewhere if something here points that way — but never towards what the parent listed to avoid. That asymmetry is deliberate.

What happens to the answer before anybody sees it

1

Parsed. JSON out of the text. Not JSON, or not an object, and it is refused.

2

Checked. shared/experience_checks.py returns a list of complaints, each naming a field and the offending value.

3

Repaired, once. ExperienceDeviser.repair() gets the complaints and is told to change what each one names. Rewording is not a repair, and the prompt says that.

4

Screened and stored. Through the gate, then written as an offer the parent has not seen yet.

The checks are where most of what this project believes lives. A way out that reaches for an object nobody was given is refused. A line one character over the display's width is refused. Ten dimensions sharing too many values with the last five afternoons is refused. None of that is style — it is the difference between an afternoon that can be run in a room and one that reads well and then fails at the printer.

2 What a moment is — the shape everything else obeys

An afternoon is a title, an overview, a length in minutes, ten dimensions it was drawn along, and a list of moments. Every moment carries the same five keys whatever it does, and then whatever its verb adds.

Three weights, one story

The same moment written at three costs, all reaching the same point. Short is about a third of standard, with what is already to hand; extended adds an optional step. The three must take different numbers of minutes. The house picks one at runtime from how much clock is left.

Four rungs of help, written in advance

A nudge inside the story, a concrete clue, an almost explicit instruction, and then the answer handed over as something the story gives. The same words are used whether somebody asked for help or whether the time simply passed — so none of them can be worded as an answer to a question.

A way out from every moment

Not one ending: a way to reach the ending from exactly here. It names a physical object the person is already holding, and those words must appear in a line of this moment or an earlier one. It never says anything is being cut short.

Nothing can be failed

There is no score, no countdown, no step that must be got right. collect branches on whether there is ink on the paper, never on whether the ink is correct. The reader is forbidden from saying so.

3 Approval — the only gate that is a person

The devised afternoon lands in the panel as something the parent has not read. They see the title, the overview, and what it will do; they approve, refuse or withdraw it.

The unread count is why nothing is devised behind the parent's back. If something is waiting for them, the house does not ask for another one. A parent who has not read the first is not handed a second to refuse.

4 Beginning — and the four verbs

On a chosen day, inside the band, with nothing already running, the house takes the oldest approved afternoon whose whole length still fits before the band closes and begins it. When one finishes early it begins the next, as long as that one fits too.

Beginning writes one file — {sheets_dir}/afternoons/{run_id}.json — and then plays moments until it reaches a collect. That file is the whole state of a running afternoon: which moment, which weight, what was printed, when help last fired, when it is over. waiting_runs() is a listing of that directory, and it is how the next minute's run knows not to start a second one.

say

Puts words on the display and waits for however long they take to read. Nothing is written to disk.

hand_over

Draws one page, prints it, and remembers the blank. If there is no printer, the pre-written instead lines go on the display and the story reaches the same point with no paper at all.

collect

Not a step that runs — a seam. The afternoon stops here and the file sits on disk until somebody presses the button.

close

Ends it on the display, from wherever it got to. An ending reached early is the same ending.

5 A page — from a few words to something on the table

The afternoon does not lay out a page. It says what the object is and what it says, and the page is drawn whole from that by an image model. Layout code for this existed and is in attic/; the decision on 24 August 2026 was one prompt, one image, the whole sheet.

The words are given, never invented

Every string that will appear has already been through the gate, and the prompt quotes it and says letter exactly this, and no other word anywhere. That is why the Italian comes back correct, accents and apostrophes included. It is not a guarantee: a map once came back with N/W/E/S on its compass rose against a line forbidding exactly those letters.

A model's white is not white

What looks like blank paper is a faint even tone, and it was more than half the ink on the page. Flattening everything above 245 took two measured pages from 2.94 % to 1.38 % and from 2.54 % to 0.97 %. Between 245 and 220 the answer moves by 0.03 points, so it is a threshold and not a knob.

The blank is kept because reading depends on it. There are no printed cells, no markers and no QR code on the sheet: the reader is given the page as printed and the page as it came back, and it says what is on the second that is not on the first.

6 A page coming back — the only branch there is

The seam ends when somebody presses the physical button. The scan takes about 29 s. Then two images go up, and what comes back decides the rest.

0 / 7
The reader describes ink, and is forbidden from describing a person. "cell 3 is empty", "a house drawn in the box on the left", "three words on the first line". It may not say how well it was done, how much effort it took, or whether anything is correct — because there is nothing here that can be got wrong. That prohibition is written into agents/page_reader.instruction.md and it is the reason the branch is marks / blank and not right / wrong.

When the plan says ask

The afternoon was written before anyone knew how it would go, and ask is the plan admitting it. The house sends the afternoon so far, which moment asked, whether a page came back, and the reader's own three words per cell — nothing else. agents/experience_continuer.py writes the moments that follow, they are checked and screened the same way, and they are appended to the run file as a segment.

A continuation gets one repair and no more. Somebody is standing at the scanner while this happens, and a second model call is another fifteen seconds. Refused twice, the afternoon takes its way out rather than making them wait.

7 The clock — help, the way out, and the ending

Every minute, a second unit looks at every run on disk and asks three questions: is a rung of help due, is it time to start leaving, and is it over. Press play to watch one afternoon.

15:00 begins · 19:00 is the end hour the parent set

Which weight

Measured against the clock left, not chosen by a person. If the standard version fits before the end hour, it runs; otherwise the short one does. Extended exists in the document and is never picked at runtime.

Help arrives on its own

Each rung has an after_minutes counted from arriving at that moment. Nobody has to ask. The rungs go up in time and the fourth hands the answer over as something the story gives.

Thirty minutes before the end

The way out for whatever moment it is on goes up, and the afternoon starts heading for its ending. It never says it is being shortened. Then the ending, then the run file and its pages are deleted.

The forgetting used to be the bug. A run that had expired was deleted in silence — measured on aft_5ec79e85, 21 August 2026: started 09:17, gone at 14:02, no ending shown to anybody. Now the expiry plays: way out, then the ending, then delete.

8 The parent while it is running

There is no progress view. No per-step timing, no help log, no list of what was answered. The person who did it is the only source, and that is a decision rather than an omission.

What the parent can do is send a typed fact. Not free text — free text would reach a model and colour what it writes. Exactly two things, both about the clock:

END_BY

The afternoon is over at this hour instead. The run file's over_at moves, and the ending arranges itself around the new one.

CLOSE_NOW

Start leaving now. The way out plays, then the ending.

A message lives one hour and is read at the top of every run, before any clock question is asked — an hour moved after that question would sit unanswered for ten minutes, and "close now" that takes ten minutes is not what those words mean.

10 Where this is weakest — read this part first

You said these need to improve a great deal. Here is what I would put on the list, from having just traced it end to end.

1 An afternoon is a straight line with one hinge

Four verbs, and only collect branches. In practice the shape is: say something, hand over a page, read it, say something else, close. The ten dimensions vary the dressing; they do not vary the structure. Two afternoons drawn along completely different dimensions still have the same skeleton.

2 The branch carries almost no information

The reader can describe eight things about the ink, and the plan is allowed to use exactly one bit of it: was there any. Everything the reader saw is passed to the continuer and then only when the plan happened to say ask. The richest signal in the system is mostly discarded.

3 Nothing survives the afternoon

The run file and its pages are deleted at the end. That is right for privacy and wrong for learning: the working rules say the system should move beyond what was chosen on what it observes, and today there is nothing left to observe from. What a system may remember about what happened, without holding a record about a person, is the open question this project has not answered.

4 One shot, and then it is fixed

The whole afternoon is written before it starts, by one call, and then only patched at ask. A model that could see the page come back and rewrite what is left would be a different system. The reason it is not that today is latency: somebody is standing at the scanner, and that is 15 s per call.

5 The devising prompt is long and does two jobs

11 608 characters, and most of them describe the JSON. The part that says what makes an afternoon worth doing is eight lines. The ratio is probably backwards, and the format could enforce more of what the prose currently asks for.

6 The seam is silent

Between printing a page and somebody pressing the button, the system does not exist. No page came back for an hour and nothing knows whether that means "still working" or "left the room". The help ladder fires blind into that gap.
